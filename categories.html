How articles and sources are sorted

Every figure on this site is grouped by categories: the subject area an article belongs to, the kind of thing it is about, and the kind of source each citation is. The categories come from Wikipedia and Wikidata, which volunteers edit, with a few rules this project adds on top. This page shows where each category comes from, each added rule with the date it was set, and the articles each rule moves.

Why this matters for the figures: A category decides which articles are compared with which. A different rule would put some articles in another group, and the group figures would change with it. The tables below list the articles a rule moves, so a reader can judge the effect.

Subject areas

In short: Which topics we cover, and how Wikipedia itself marks them.

Two sources. Contentious-topic notices are placed on talk pages under Wikipedia's arbitration and community decisions, and mark subject areas where editing is under special rules. WikiProject banners are placed by any editor who judges that an article falls under a group's subject; they are used only for the subjects that have no notice (marked “chosen by subject”). Movements that Wikidata also records as organisations

In short: Our rule for things Wikidata calls both a movement and an organisation.

Wikidata records many social and political movements and ideologies (socialism, anarchism, the Cultural Revolution) as organisations as well as movements. Rule, from 5 November 2026: an item in both classes stays with organisations only when Wikidata gives it a headquarters, a chairperson or a member count; otherwise it is placed with ideas and movements. Before that date every such item counted as an organisation. Under the rule, Hamas and the Taliban, which have a headquarters and a chairperson in Wikidata, stay organisations.

Journalists and commentators

In short: Our rule for who counts as a journalist or commentator.

Wikidata lists journalism among the occupations of many people known mainly for something else; Karl Marx, Mahatma Gandhi and Benito Mussolini all wrote for newspapers. Rule, from 5 November 2026: a person counts as a journalist or commentator when Wikidata lists one of the occupations journalist, columnist, political commentator, news presenter or pundit, and Wikidata's one-line English description of them names such a role (journalist, columnist, commentator, pundit, reporter, correspondent, news presenter or anchor). Others are placed with other public figures. Before that date the occupation alone counted. The description is itself written by volunteers.

Oddities in Wikipedia's own sorting

In short: Places where Wikipedia's own tags and categories are surprising or inconsistent, with links so you can check.

Found while choosing the subject areas: places where Wikipedia's notices and WikiProject banners do not sort articles the way their names suggest. Noted by hand while choosing subject areas; each entry links the talk-page revision read. Banners and notices are edited over time, so a later revision may differ.

  • Liberalism. The talk pages of Liberalism and Classical liberalism carry the banner of WikiProject Libertarianism. There is no WikiProject Liberalism, so this project's liberalism coverage comes from the Libertarianism banner. Read: Talk:Liberalism (2026-10-13), Talk:Classical liberalism (2026-08-22).
  • Postmodernism and Michel Foucault. Neither talk page carries the WikiProject Philosophy banner. Postmodernism carries the banners of WikiProjects Culture, Skepticism and Visual arts; Michel Foucault those of Socialism and Libertarianism, among others. Neither fits a subject area here, so Michel Foucault is listed under socialism by his banner and Postmodernism is added on request. Read: Talk:Postmodernism (2026-08-20), Talk:Michel Foucault (2026-10-17).
  • Antisemitism. The article Antisemitism carries no contentious-topic notice, while New antisemitism carries the Arab–Israeli one. Wikipedia's only contentious topic named for antisemitism is Antisemitism in Poland. Read: Talk:Antisemitism (2026-09-29), Talk:New antisemitism (2026-09-20), Talk:Antisemitism in Poland (2026-05-08).
  • Antisemitism in Poland. Wikipedia's Antisemitism in Poland notice also sits on Adolf Hitler, Auschwitz concentration camp and other Second World War and Holocaust articles, so that area is wider than its name. Read: Talk:Adolf Hitler (2026-10-31), Talk:Auschwitz concentration camp (2026-10-21).
  • Holocaust denial. The article Holocaust denial carries no contentious-topic notice. Its banners include WikiProjects Alternative views and Skepticism. Read: Talk:Holocaust denial (2026-11-02).
  • The far right. Far-right politics, Fascism and White nationalism carry no contentious-topic notice. Alt-right and Great Replacement conspiracy theory carry the US politics notice, and Alt-right also the banner of WikiProject Presidents of the United States. Read: Talk:Far-right politics (2026-10-12), Talk:Fascism (2026-10-12), Talk:White nationalism (2026-07-29), Talk:Alt-right (2026-08-21), Talk:Great Replacement conspiracy theory (2026-11-01).
  • Critical theory and Cultural Marxism. Critical theory and Cultural Marxism conspiracy theory carry the WikiProject Socialism banner, so they fall in the socialism subject; the second also carries the Conservatism banner, so it falls in both. Read: Talk:Critical theory (2026-09-19), Talk:Cultural Marxism conspiracy theory (2026-10-27).
  • Buildings, figures, religions and campaigns filed as organisations (Wikidata). Wikidata's classes place Hagia Sophia, the Dome of the Rock, Cain and Abel, Sharia, Muslims, the Spanish Inquisition and Persecution of Uyghurs in China under organisation, so they appear in the Islam list's organisations. In the China list the same happens to Buddhism (through “major religious group”) and the Great Leap Forward (through “campaign”). No rule moves them: they are kept as Wikidata files them, because a rule for buildings, religions and campaigns would need its own class list and would move other articles too. If such a rule is added, this note and the Categories page will give its date and the articles it moves. Read: Wikidata: Hagia Sophia (2026-11-05), Wikidata: Dome of the Rock (2026-11-05), Wikidata: Cain and Abel (2026-11-05), Wikidata: Sharia (2026-11-05), Wikidata: Buddhism (2026-11-06), Wikidata: Great Leap Forward (2026-11-06).
  • Religious communities filed as movements (Wikidata). The Shakers and Peoples Temple are recorded in Wikidata as movements as well as organisations, with no headquarters, chairperson or member count, so under this project's movement rule they are placed with ideas and movements. Read: Wikidata: Shakers (2026-11-05), Wikidata: Peoples Temple (2026-11-05).
  • WikiProject Islam banners. The talk pages of Jesus, Rita Hayworth, MF Doom, Cat Stevens and Balinese Hinduism carry the WikiProject Islam banner, so they are in the Islam subject. Read: Talk:Jesus (2026-10-28), Talk:Rita Hayworth (2026-05-15), Talk:MF Doom (2026-11-03), Talk:Cat Stevens (2026-09-19), Talk:Balinese Hinduism (2026-07-12).

How cited sources are sorted

In short: How the sources behind footnotes are sorted into kinds.

  • Web address to source. A citation's web address is reduced to its registrable domain using the Public Suffix List, so news.bbc.co.uk and www.bbc.co.uk are one source, while blogspot.com subdomains stay separate sites.
  • Wikipedia's rating. The perennial sources list, kept by Wikipedia editors through discussion; it covers a few hundred of the most-discussed sources.
  • Kind of source (newspaper, publisher, NGO, government body ...), from the source's Wikidata classes, in the same way as article kinds above.
  • Media Bias/Fact Check labels and JUFO academic levels, each rater's own scheme, shown under its own name.
  • Books are grouped by the agency that issued the ISBN, which reflects the language area or country where the ISBN was registered, not necessarily where the publisher works.

The Sources page has the full description of each.
