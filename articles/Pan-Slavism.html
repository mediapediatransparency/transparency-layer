Pan-Slavism, a movement which crystallised in the mid-19th century, aimed at unity of all the Slavic peoples. Its main impact occurred in the Balkans, where non-Slavic empires - the Byzantine Empire, Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire, and Venice - had ruled the South Slavs for centuries. It was also used as a political tool by both the Russian Empire and the Soviet Union, the latter of which gained political-military influence and control over all Slavic-majority nations between 1945 and 1948.
