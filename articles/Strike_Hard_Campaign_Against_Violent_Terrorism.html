In May 2014, China launched the "Strike Hard Campaign against Violent Terrorism" (; 2014-present) in the far west province of Xinjiang. It is an aspect of the Xinjiang conflict, the ongoing struggle by the Chinese government to effectively manage the ethnically diverse and tumultuous province. This campaign has been credited as effectively giving the state broad latitude to punish and control Muslim Uyghurs based on their religious identities. China has used the global “war on terror” of the 2000s to frame separatist and ethnic unrest into acts of Islamist terrorism to legitimize increasingly repressive counter-insurgency policies in Xinjiang. One element of this campaign has been the jailing of nearly one million Uyghurs in Xinjiang's re-education camps. Chinese officials have maintained that the campaign is essential for national security purposes.
