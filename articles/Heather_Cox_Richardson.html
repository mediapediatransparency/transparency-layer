How the summary rows are calculated. For the sources in scope that have a score s (independent quality, 0 to 1; or audience leaning, −1 to +1), each cited c times: mean per source = (s1 + … + sn) ÷ n, each source counted once; mean per citation = (c1·s1 + … + cn·sn) ÷ (c1 + … + cn), each source counted as often as it is cited. Both cover only scored sources: the share of citations they cover is given beside them. The rows cover every citation in scope, not only the sources listed in the table.

About the ratings: Wikipedia's own list (Perennial sources) rates about 530 sources that its editors argue about often. Most sources are not on it; that is not a bad mark.
  • The quality score (Lin and colleagues, 2023) runs from 0 to 1 and covers news websites. Books and journals have no score.
  • Audience leaning says who shares a website, not what it publishes: −1 means only Democrats shared it, +1 only Republicans, 0 both equally. Out of every 100 people sharing it, Republicans = (1 + score) ÷ 2 × 100, so −0.4 is about 70 Democrats to 30 Republicans. It comes from US Twitter users matched to voter records (DomainDemo, 2025; Robertson and colleagues, 2018) and covers only websites shared widely enough.
  • Few publishers have a recorded political affiliation, and those records are written by volunteers on Wikidata and Wikipedia.
  • The ratings are today's ratings, not the ratings when the footnote was added.
  • Links between organisations (owner, founder, member of) come from Wikidata, which anyone can edit. † marks a fact for which Wikidata gives no source.
