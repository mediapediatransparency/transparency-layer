Neoliberalism or neo-liberalism is the 20th-century resurgence of 19th-century ideas associated with laissez-faire economic liberalism and free market capitalism. Those ideas include economic liberalization policies such as privatization, austerity, deregulation, free trade and reductions in government spending in order to increase the role of the private sector in the economy and society. Short description: Political philosophy supporting economic liberalization

Neoliberalism (also neo-liberalism) is a term used to signify the late 20th century political reappearance of 19th-century ideas associated with free-market capitalism after it fell into decline following the Second World War. A prominent factor in the rise of conservative and libertarian organizations, political parties, and think tanks, and predominantly advocated by them, it is generally associated with policies of economic liberalization, including privatization, deregulation, globalization, free trade, monetarism, austerity, and reductions in government spending in order to increase the role of the private sector in the economy and society. The defining features of neoliberalism in both thought and practice have been the subject of substantial scholarly debate.

Short description: Political ideology promoting free-market capitalism

Neoliberalism is a political and economic ideology that advocates for free-market capitalism and developed from challenges to classical liberalism in the early 20th century. It advocates policies that became dominant from the late 20th century that promote market processes and competition. The term has multiple, competing definitions, and is often used pejoratively. In scholarly use, the term is often used to describe a multitude of phenomena, but can be used to contrast with other developments in liberalism like Ordoliberalism, New liberalism (ideology) or Social Liberalism. It is primarily employed to delineate the societal transformation resulting from market-based reforms.
