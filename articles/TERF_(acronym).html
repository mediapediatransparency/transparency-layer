Short description: Acronym for "trans-exclusionary radical feminist"

TERF () is an acronym for trans-exclusionary radical feminist. First recorded in 2008, the term was originally used to distinguish trans-inclusive feminists from a group of radical feminists who reject the assertion that trans women are women, the inclusion of trans women in women's spaces, and transgender rights legislation. Trans-inclusive feminists assert that these ideas are transphobic. Use of the term TERF has since broadened to include reference to people with trans-exclusionary views who are not necessarily involved with radical feminism.

Short description: Acronym for trans-exclusionary radical feminist

TERF () is an acronym for trans-exclusionary radical feminist. First recorded in 2008, the term TERF was originally used to distinguish transgender-inclusive feminists from a group of radical feminists who reject the position that trans women are women, reject the inclusion of trans women in women's spaces, and oppose transgender rights legislation. About the ratings: Wikipedia's own list (Perennial sources) rates about 530 sources that its editors argue about often. Most sources are not on it; that is not a bad mark.
  • The quality score (Lin and colleagues, 2023) runs from 0 to 1 and covers news websites. Books and journals have no score.
  • Audience leaning says who shares a website, not what it publishes: −1 means only Democrats shared it, +1 only Republicans, 0 both equally. Out of every 100 people sharing it, Republicans = (1 + score) ÷ 2 × 100, so −0.4 is about 70 Democrats to 30 Republicans. It comes from US Twitter users matched to voter records (DomainDemo, 2025; Robertson and colleagues, 2018) and covers only websites shared widely enough.
  • Few publishers have a recorded political affiliation, and those records are written by volunteers on Wikidata and Wikipedia.
  • The ratings are today's ratings, not the ratings when the footnote was added.
