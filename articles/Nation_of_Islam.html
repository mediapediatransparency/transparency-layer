The Nation of Islam, abbreviated as NOI, is an African American political and religious movement, founded in Detroit, Michigan, United States, by Wallace D. Fard Muhammad on July 4, 1930. Its stated goals are to improve the spiritual, mental, social, and economic condition of African Americans in the United States and all of humanity. Critics have described the organization as being black supremacist and antisemitic. The Southern Poverty Law Center tracks the NOI as a hate group. Its official newspaper is The Final Call. The Nation of Islam (NOI) is a religious and political organization founded in the United States by Wallace Fard Muhammad in 1930. A black nationalist organization, the NOI focuses its attention on the African diaspora, especially on African Americans. While it identifies itself as promoting a form of Islam, its beliefs differ considerably from mainstream Islamic traditions. Scholars of religion characterize it as a new religious movement. How the summary rows are calculated. For the sources in scope that have a score s (independent quality, 0 to 1; or audience leaning, −1 to +1), each cited c times: mean per source = (s1 + … + sn) ÷ n, each source counted once; mean per citation = (c1·s1 + … + cn·sn) ÷ (c1 + … + cn), each source counted as often as it is cited. Both cover only scored sources: the share of citations they cover is given beside them. The rows cover every citation in scope, not only the sources listed in the table.

About the ratings: Wikipedia's own list (Perennial sources) rates about 530 sources that its editors argue about often. Most sources are not on it; that is not a bad mark.
  • The quality score (Lin and colleagues, 2023) runs from 0 to 1 and covers news websites. Books and journals have no score.
  • Audience leaning says who shares a website, not what it publishes: −1 means only Democrats shared it, +1 only Republicans, 0 both equally. Out of every 100 people sharing it, Republicans = (1 + score) ÷ 2 × 100, so −0.4 is about 70 Democrats to 30 Republicans. It comes from US Twitter users matched to voter records (DomainDemo, 2025; Robertson and colleagues, 2018) and covers only websites shared widely enough.
  • Few publishers have a recorded political affiliation, and those records are written by volunteers on Wikidata and Wikipedia.
  • The ratings are today's ratings, not the ratings when the footnote was added.
