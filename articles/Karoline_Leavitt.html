Wikipedia's voice in the opening

Each sentence of the opening section checked against Wikipedia's neutrality policy. A sentence is marked (orange edge) when there is evidence it is contested (editors took it out for a day or more and put it back, tagged it as disputed, or argued over it on the talk page; or it uses a word from Wikipedia's own “Words to watch” guideline) and it is stated in Wikipedia's voice, naming no one who holds the view. That marks sentences to check against the rule, not a finding that a sentence breaks it. “With attribution” names the cited authors in front of the sentence by a fixed template; the last column shows the earlier wording of the same sentence as Wikipedia printed it on 1 January of 2015, 2019, 2023, with the works it cited then that are gone now. How the summary rows are calculated. For the sources in scope that have a score s (independent quality, 0 to 1; or audience leaning, −1 to +1), each cited c times: mean per source = (s1 + … + sn) ÷ n, each source counted once; mean per citation = (c1·s1 + … + cn·sn) ÷ (c1 + … + cn), each source counted as often as it is cited.
