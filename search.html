Searches article titles (analysed, and waiting to be analysed), topic names, sources cited in at least two articles, and the pages of this site. Capitals, accents and dashes don't matter. Every word you type must appear.

Track words in an article over time

Pick a Wikipedia article. We fetch it as it stood on 1 January and 1 July of each year and count how often words appear, per 1,000 words of text. First we list the six words whose use changed most between the first and the last version (three up, three down), picked by a fixed statistical test. You can chart those, or up to six words or phrases of your own. A count only says how often a word appears, not what the article says about it.

The opening on a date you choose

The first paragraph as it read on the date you pick, next to the latest version. Struck through: taken out since. Underlined: added since.

How it counts: footnotes, tables, templates and hidden notes are removed first, so only the readable text is counted (roughly; a few template words may remain). Capitals do not matter. A phrase matches whole words only, so “colonial” does not match “colonialism”: enter both if you want both. Words that changed most: every word of three letters or more, except common words such as “the” and “which”, that appears at least ten times in the first and last versions together, ranked by a standard test of change in proportions (the log odds ratio divided by its standard error), so a word going from 20 to 60 mentions counts for more than one going from 1 to 3. The versions are fetched from Wikipedia by your browser when you press Track; nothing is stored.
