From Wikipedia's public records

Who wrote what you read on Wikipedia?

Wikipedia shows you what an article says today, but not how it came to say it. We work that out from Wikipedia's own public records, with a link to the evidence for every number.

What each article page shows

Four questions, each answered from the record, with a link to the evidence for every number.

1 · The opening

Does it name who holds contested views, or state one view in Wikipedia's own voice?

The first paragraph in 2015, 2019, 2023 and today, side by side: what changed, which sources were taken out, and which sentences state contested views without saying whose they are.

How it is measured
2 · What it rests on

How authoritative are its sources, what leanings are recorded for them, and can a reader check them?

Each source with Wikipedia's own reliability rating, academic level, outside raters' scores, recorded political labels, and whether the footnote can be checked.

How it is measured
3 · Who wrote it

Who wrote the words you are reading, and what does Wikipedia record about them?

How concentrated the writing is, how much is by editors Wikipedia later sanctioned, interests editors declared on Wikipedia, and where else they write. Editors are never named.

How it is measured
4 · How it changes

What was fought over, and what kept text in place?

How often it is edited, what was taken out and put back, and the rules applied.

How it is measured

We do not judge whether an article is right, or whether its editors are good or bad. We show where the record and Wikipedia's own standards can be set side by side.
